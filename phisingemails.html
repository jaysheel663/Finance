What is a Phishing Email?

A phishing email is a fake email designed to trick people into clicking malicious links,downloading infected files or revealing confidential information such as passwords,OTPs and banking details.

Think Before You Click

Phishing emails often look exactly like genuine emails from trusted organizations,making them difficult to identify.

Cyber criminals create a sense of urgency so that users click links without verifying the sender.

Remember

Never click unknown email links or open unexpected attachments.

Real Phishing Email Examples

These are some of the most common phishing emails reported by users.

Fake Bank Email

Emails claiming your bank account will be blocked unless you verify your details immediately.

Fake KYC Update

Fraudsters send emails requesting KYC updates with links to fake banking websites.

Income Tax Refund

Fake emails promise tax refunds and ask users to submit banking credentials.

Courier Delivery Email

Fake parcel delivery emails ask users to click tracking links that install malware or steal passwords.

How Phishing Emails Work

Cyber criminals carefully design phishing emails to appear genuine so victims unknowingly reveal sensitive information.

Fake Email Sender

Attackers create email addresses that closely resemble official company email IDs.

Malicious Links

Clicking the link opens a fake website designed to steal usernames,passwords and banking details.

Dangerous Attachments

Some phishing emails contain infected documents or files that install malware on your device.

Creates Urgency

Messages often say "Your account will be blocked"or "Respond within 24 hours."

Warning Signs

Look for these warning signs before trusting any email.

Unknown Email Address

The sender's email doesn't exactly match the official company domain.

Grammar Mistakes

Many phishing emails contain spelling mistakes and poor grammar.

Urgent Threats

Emails demanding immediate action are often designed to create panic.

How to Stay Safe from Phishing Emails

Following these simple safety practices can help you avoid phishing attacks and protect your personal and financial information.

Always verify the sender's email address before opening attachments or clicking any links.

Type official website URLs directly into your browser instead of clicking email links.

Enable Two-Factor Authentication(2FA)on your important accounts for additional security.

Keep your antivirus software and operating system updated to detect malicious files.

Common Mistakes People Make

Many phishing attacks succeed because users unknowingly make these mistakes.

Clicking Unknown Links

Clicking suspicious email links without checking the website address.

Opening Unknown Files

Downloading attachments from unknown senders that may contain malware.

Sharing Passwords

Entering login credentials on fake websites that look identical to official websites.

Ignoring Security Warnings

Ignoring browser or email security alerts before visiting suspicious pages.

What To Do If You Clicked a Phishing Email

Act immediately if you think you've opened a phishing link or attachment.

Disconnect Internet

Disconnect your device from the internet if malware may have been downloaded.

Change Passwords

Immediately change the passwords of affected accounts from a trusted device.

Run Antivirus Scan

Perform a complete scan to detect and remove malicious software.

Inform Your Bank

If banking credentials were entered,contact your bank immediately and secure your account.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can phishing emails look exactly like genuine emails?

Yes.Many phishing emails closely imitate official companies,including their logos and formatting.

Is every attachment in an email safe?

No.Only open attachments from trusted senders after verifying their authenticity.

What should I do if I entered my password on a fake website?

Change your password immediately,enable Two-Factor Authentication and monitor your account for suspicious activity.
